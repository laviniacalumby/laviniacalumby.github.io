IIN Philosophy
Your Health Is Bigger Than Food
Before we talk about what you eat, we need to talk about everything else. Because what you eat is only one piece of your health, and it is never the whole story.

Primary Foods: What Truly Nourishes You

One of the principles I work from is the concept of primary and secondary foods, a framework from the Institute for Integrative Nutrition. Secondary foods are what we eat: proteins, carbohydrates, vegetables, fats. They matter enormously. But primary foods are the things that nourish us at a deeper level, and they have just as much influence on how our body looks, feels, and performs as anything on our plate.

Primary foods include: your relationships and the quality of your connections. Your career and whether your work gives you a sense of purpose. Your movement and daily activity beyond the gym. Your creativity and joy, the things that make you feel alive. Your stress levels and how supported you feel. Your sleep and rest. Your spiritual or inner life, whatever that means to you.

Why this matters for your goals: chronic stress raises cortisol, which directly promotes fat storage and muscle breakdown regardless of how well you eat. Unfulfilling work or strained relationships create a physiological stress response that no amount of protein can undo. Poor sleep disrupts the hormones that regulate hunger, recovery, and body composition more than almost any nutritional factor. Your body does not separate your emotional life from your physical one. Neither should your approach to health.

As you work through this guide, I want you to hold both. Notice what is nourishing you beyond food. Notice where you feel depleted in life, not just in the gym. Those areas are part of your health plan too, and we can explore them in our check-ins together.

Bio-Individuality: You Are the Expert on Your Body

Another principle I hold strongly is bio-individuality: the idea that there is no one-size-fits-all approach to nutrition. What works beautifully for one woman may not work for another, even two women of the same age, weight, and goals. Your body is unique. Your digestion, your hormonal profile, your stress patterns, your food history, and your relationship with eating are all yours alone.

Everything in this guide is based on research and on what tends to support women with similar goals. But I want you to use it as a starting point for your own awareness, not as a prescription. Pay attention to how your body responds. Notice your energy, your digestion, your mood, your sleep, and your hunger. Your body is giving you information constantly. Learning to read those signals is more valuable in the long run than following any specific set of guidelines, including these ones.

If something in this guide does not feel right for you or your lifestyle, tell me. We will adjust it. The goal is a way of eating that is genuinely yours, sustainable, and that supports the fullest version of your life.

One of the most common patterns I notice in women who train consistently but are not seeing the results they want is that food gets built around everything except protein. 01 Muscle Building

Builds and Repairs

Every time you train you create tiny tears in your muscle fibers. Protein provides the amino acids your body uses to repair and rebuild those fibers larger and stronger. Without enough protein, you train but you do not build.

02 Hormones

Regulates Hormones

Estrogen, progesterone, cortisol, and insulin are all directly influenced by what and when you eat. Chronic undereating raises cortisol and disrupts estrogen. Consistent protein supports the hormonal balance that keeps your cycle regular and your energy stable.

Post-training nutrition is the signal that tells your body to shift from breaking down to building up. Carbohydrates replenish energy. Protein starts repair. Together they reduce soreness, restore strength faster, and prepare you for the next session.

What you eat in the evening directly affects sleep quality. Adequate carbohydrates raise serotonin, a precursor to melatonin. Magnesium-rich foods support sleep depth. Undereating in the evening raises nighttime cortisol and disrupts the sleep architecture that is essential for muscle repair.

Understanding Protein
Protein. Simply Explained.
Of all the nutritional factors connected to body composition and muscle building, protein is the one that appears most consistently in the research. Here is what the evidence suggests and how to think about it practically.

How Much Tends to Support Your Goals

Research on body recomposition and muscle building in active women generally points to a range of 1.6 to 2g of protein per kilogram of bodyweight per day as a useful target. This is educational context, not a clinical prescription, and your individual needs may differ based on your digestion, food preferences, stress levels, and how your body responds.

As a reference point based on your current weight: the research-backed range would suggest somewhere around 82g per day at the lower end and closer to 100g per day at the higher end. Spread across 3 to 4 meals, that translates to roughly 20 to 30g of protein per meal, which tends to be very manageable once you understand what it looks like on a plate. These are reference points to explore, not targets to stress over.

A simple starting point if numbers feel overwhelming: aim to include a palm-sized portion of a protein-rich food at every meal. A palm of chicken, fish, eggs, or Greek yogurt tends to provide around 20 to 30g of protein and is a practical way to build awareness without counting.

Why Many Active Women Under-Eat Protein

Healthy diets are often naturally rich in vegetables, whole grains, fruits, and healthy fats, all of which are excellent for your health. But none of them are high in protein. It is entirely possible to eat very well and still be eating less protein than your training demands. That gap between what your body is asking for and what it is receiving is often where the missing results live. Awareness is the first step.

When You Eat Matters
Nutrition Timing Around Training
You do not need to obsess over timing. But research suggests that a few simple habits around your training windows tend to support better recovery and results. These are ideas to explore, not rules to follow perfectly.
Before Training

Many women find a light meal or snack with some protein and carbohydrates 30 to 60 minutes before training helps sustain energy through the session. Greek yogurt and fruit, or a small rice and chicken portion from your prep, tend to work well. Notice what feels right for your body and digestion.

During Training

Staying hydrated throughout your session supports performance and recovery. Sipping water regularly is a simple habit that makes a difference. On longer or back-to-back training days, adding a pinch of sea salt to your water can help maintain electrolyte balance.

After Training

Eating a combination of protein and carbohydrates after training tends to support muscle repair and recovery. Many women find that doing this within 30 to 45 minutes of finishing their session works well. A protein shake with fruit, or a simple bowl of chicken and rice, are practical options.

Before Bed

Some research suggests that eating a slow-digesting protein source in the evening, such as cottage cheese or Greek yogurt, may support overnight muscle repair, particularly on training days. This is something to experiment with and see if it improves your recovery and sleep quality.

Your Goal Explained
Body Recomposition: What It Actually Means
You want to lose the fat that has accumulated, particularly around your hips, while building lean muscle. This is body recomposition and understanding it changes how you think about food entirely.

Why the Scale Is the Wrong Measure

Body recomposition means losing fat and gaining muscle simultaneously. When this happens, your weight on the scale may not change much, even as your body changes significantly. Muscle is denser than fat. You can lose 3kg of fat and gain 2kg of muscle and the scale shows only 1kg difference, while your body looks and feels completely different. Do not use the scale as your primary measure of progress. Use: how your clothes fit, your strength in the gym (tracked weekly), your energy levels, and the measurements Lavinia takes at check-ins.

The recomposition equation for you specifically: eat at your maintenance calories, hit your protein target every day, and train with progressive overload 4 days per week. At 51kg and 5'5", you are already lean. Your goal is not to eat less. It is to eat better, with enough protein to build muscle and enough total food to fuel the training that changes your body composition. Restricting calories at your weight would work against you, not for you.

What Is Causing the Hip Fat

At 51kg and 5'5", the changes you have noticed around your hips over the past year are not the result of eating too much. They are the result of a hormonal shift. At 38, estrogen begins its first gradual natural decline. Estrogen actively directs fat storage toward the hips and thighs during the reproductive years as a biological reserve. As it begins to decline, the body's fat distribution pattern starts to shift and remodel, even without any change in diet or activity. This is why women who have always been slim suddenly notice changes in specific areas at this age. It is not a failure. It is your hormones changing the rules. The evidence-backed response is resistance training with progressive overload combined with adequate protein and consistent total food intake. Building lean muscle raises your resting metabolic rate, improves insulin sensitivity, directly signals the body to shift from fat storage to fat utilization, and counteracts the hormonal redistribution you are experiencing. You do not need to eat less. You need to eat smarter and train harder.

Food and Female Physiology
How Food Supports Your Hormones
At 38, with a regular cycle and an active lifestyle, the foods you eat directly influence your estrogen metabolism, progesterone levels, cortisol, and insulin sensitivity. Here is what actually matters.

Protein Is Not Just for Muscle. It Builds Your Hormones.

This is the connection most nutrition guides miss entirely. Protein is not only the raw material for muscle. It is the raw material for hormones themselves. Amino acids from dietary protein are the direct building blocks of thyroid hormones (which regulate metabolism, energy, and body weight), neurotransmitters including serotonin, dopamine, and GABA (which regulate mood, sleep, and stress response), growth hormone (which drives fat metabolism and muscle repair overnight), and peptide hormones including insulin and glucagon which control blood sugar and energy storage. Without adequate protein, your body cannot synthesize these hormones in sufficient quantities regardless of what else you eat. This is not a deficiency you can supplement your way out of. It requires consistent daily protein from food.

What this means practically: every time you under-eat protein, you are not just limiting muscle building. You are limiting your body's capacity to regulate your thyroid, your mood, your sleep, your metabolism, and your blood sugar simultaneously. Protein is the most hormonally relevant macronutrient in your diet. This is why it comes first.

IIN Principle
Food Combining: How Foods Work Together
Food combining is the understanding that certain foods eaten together create significantly better outcomes than the same foods eaten separately. Not because of digestion myths, but because of nutrient absorption, blood sugar management, and how macronutrients interact in the body.

Combinations That Amplify Results

Protein + Fat

Slows digestion and increases satiety. Stabilizes blood sugar and prevents the energy crashes that drive cravings. Example: eggs with avocado. Greek yogurt with nuts. Chicken with olive oil.

Protein + Carb (around training)

The most important combination for body recomposition. Carbohydrates create an insulin response that drives amino acids into muscle tissue. This combination post-training is what makes the training stimulus translate into actual muscle. Example: rice and chicken. Banana and protein shake.

Iron + Vitamin C

Vitamin C increases non-heme iron absorption by up to 3 times. Especially important during menstruation. Example: lentils with red pepper. Spinach salad with lemon dressing. Red meat with tomatoes.

Fat-Soluble Vitamins + Fat

Vitamins A, D, E, and K require dietary fat to be absorbed. Eating vegetables without fat means many of their nutrients pass through unabsorbed. Example: always add olive oil, avocado, or nuts to your vegetable dishes. Never eat a fat-free salad.

Turmeric + Black Pepper

Piperine in black pepper increases curcumin absorption from turmeric by up to 2000%. Always pair them. Add both to rice, soups, roasted vegetables, or scrambled eggs. This combination actively reduces post-training inflammation.

Plant Protein + Grains

Most plant proteins are incomplete amino acid sources. Combining legumes with grains creates a complete profile. Example: lentils with rice. Hummus with pita. Black beans with quinoa. Essential if you have plant-based meals.

Magnesium + Vitamin B6

B6 enhances magnesium absorption and together they support progesterone production, reduce PMS symptoms, and improve sleep quality. Example: chicken (B6) with pumpkin seeds or dark chocolate (magnesium). Banana and almonds as a snack.

What to Avoid Combining

Coffee with iron-rich meals reduces iron absorption significantly. Leave 30 minutes between coffee and iron-rich food. Calcium supplements with iron supplements compete for absorption. Take them at different times of day.

The Foundation of Everything
Gut Health and Microbiome
Your gut microbiome is a community of trillions of bacteria that directly controls estrogen metabolism, inflammation, immune function, mood, sleep quality, and how efficiently your body absorbs the nutrients you eat. This is not wellness language. It is physiology. A healthy gut makes everything else work better.

Why Your Gut Matters for Your Goals Specifically

The gut microbiome produces an enzyme called beta-glucuronidase which is responsible for reactivating and recirculating estrogen through the body. When gut bacteria are imbalanced a condition called dysbiosis this enzyme becomes overactive and estrogen recirculates instead of being cleared. The result is estrogen dominance: more fat storage around the hips and midsection, heavier and more symptomatic periods, mood disruption, and difficulty losing fat despite clean eating. This is one of the most common and most overlooked reasons women at 38 struggle with body composition despite doing everything right.

The connection to your goal is direct: a healthy gut clears estrogen efficiently, reduces inflammation, improves insulin sensitivity, and allows your body to absorb the protein and micronutrients your training depends on. You can eat a perfect diet and still underperform if your gut is not functioning well.

Building Lean Muscle
Nutrition for Hypertrophy
Hypertrophy means building lean muscle tissue. Your training creates the stimulus. Your nutrition provides the raw material. Without both working together, only one of them is doing its job. Here is exactly what your body needs to build the lean, defined physique you are working toward.

The Three Non-Negotiables for Muscle Building

01 Protein Synthesis

Leucine Threshold

Muscle protein synthesis requires a minimum dose of leucine, an essential amino acid, to be triggered. This threshold is approximately 2.5 to 3g of leucine per meal. A 30g serving of animal protein (chicken, fish, eggs, Greek yogurt) meets this threshold. This is why protein dose per meal matters, not just total daily intake.

02 Energy Availability

Do Not Under-Eat

Muscle cannot be built in a caloric deficit. Restriction tells your body to preserve fat and burn muscle for energy, the opposite of your goal. At 51kg you do not need to eat less. You need to eat enough to fuel training and support recovery. If you are losing weight unintentionally while training, you are under-eating and muscle building will stall.

03 Recovery Nutrition

The Repair Window

The 2 to 3 hours after training are when your body is most primed to absorb protein and begin muscle repair. A protein and carbohydrate meal within 45 minutes of finishing your session is the single most impactful nutritional habit you can build around your training.

5 Sauces and Dressings That Make Everything Taste Different

One of the simplest ways to create variety without creativity is to rotate your sauces. The same chicken and rice bowl tastes completely different with each of these.

SauceHow to Make It (30 seconds)Pairs With
Lemon Tahini2 tbsp tahini, juice of 1 lemon, 1 tbsp water, pinch of salt. Stir.Any grain bowl, roasted vegetables, chicken, falafel
Ginger Soy2 tbsp soy sauce, 1 tsp sesame oil, 1 tsp grated ginger, squeeze of limeSalmon, shrimp, tofu, rice, stir-fried vegetables
Avocado LimeHalf an avocado mashed with lime juice, salt, and chili flakesEggs, tuna, grilled chicken, anything Mexican-inspired
Yogurt Herb4 tbsp Greek yogurt, lemon juice, garlic powder, fresh or dried herbs (dill, mint, or parsley)Salmon, grilled vegetables, falafel, lamb
PestoBuy good quality store-bought. One jar lasts a week.Pasta, rice, chicken, eggs, roasted tomatoes, anything

The variety formula: same protein, same base, different sauce and different color vegetables. Monday chicken bowl with lemon tahini and roasted peppers. Wednesday the same chicken bowl with ginger soy and spinach and cucumber. Thursday the same bowl with avocado lime and tomatoes and corn. Three different meals from one Sunday prep session.

IIN Philosophy
Your Health Is Multidimensional
Food supports your goals. But your goals are supported by much more than food alone.

Everything That Affects How You Feel

The research is consistent: sleep quality directly regulates the hormones that control hunger, recovery, and body composition. Chronic stress raises cortisol, disrupts estrogen metabolism, and promotes the exact pattern of fat storage you are experiencing, regardless of how well you eat. The quality of your relationships and whether your daily life feels meaningful influence your nervous system, your inflammation levels, and your capacity to recover from training in ways that no supplement can replicate.

This is not to say food does not matter. It matters enormously. But the most effective nutritional strategy in the world has a ceiling when the rest of life is depleting you. As your coach, I am interested in the whole picture, not just what is on your plate.

The questions worth asking alongside your nutrition: How is your sleep? How is your stress, really? Are there areas of your life that feel draining rather than nourishing? Are you moving joyfully, not just training hard? Do you have relationships and moments in your day that genuinely restore you? These are not separate from your health goals. They are part of them.

In our Friday check-ins, feel free to share more than just your weights and energy score. If something in your life feels off, that context helps me support you better. Health is not built in the gym and kitchen alone. It is built across the whole of how you live.
