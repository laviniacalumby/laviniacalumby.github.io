Before You Start

Kathy, you are a businesswoman running a company, with two kids, and managing everything that comes with life in Manhattan. Your schedule is relentless and your stress is real. This program is built around that truth. You do not need to read this entire document at once. Open it each morning before your session, find your day, and follow it. That is all.

01 Warm Up First

Every session starts with 3 to 5 minutes on the treadmill or bike before anything else. You are training at 6am. Your body needs this especially your joints and connective tissue.

02 Low Energy Days

If you slept under 4 hours or feel genuinely depleted, do your activation and 20 minutes easy cardio only and go home. One modified session protects weeks of progress. Do not push through real exhaustion.

03 Check In Every Friday

Send me a quick message or voice note: your energy this week out of 10, your key weights, and one thing that felt hard or that you have a question about. That is it. This keeps everything on track.

The One Rule Inside the Gym

You spend your days split across ten things at once. The gym is the one place where that changes. When you walk in, your job, your phone, your mental to-do list, none of it belongs here. This hour is yours. It is also the mechanism through which everything you want from this program actually happens.

Put the Phone Away

Not face down. Away. Every time you pick it up between sets, your nervous system re-enters work mode and cortisol rises. You already walk in with cortisol elevated from stress and poor sleep. The gym is where you lower it, not maintain it. Your phone can wait 50 minutes.

Mind-Muscle Connection

The research is clear: consciously thinking about the muscle you are training during each rep increases activation by up to 60%. This is not motivational language, it is neuroscience. When you do a hip thrust, feel your glute contract. When you row, feel your back. Slow down enough to feel it. That is where the results live.

Follow the Rest Times

Every session has rest times written in for a reason. They are not suggestions. Too little rest and your muscles cannot generate the force needed for the next set, you are training fatigue instead of strength. Too much rest and you lose the metabolic and hormonal benefits of the session. Set a timer. Rest exactly as written. Then go.

Be Here

You have 45 to 55 minutes. That is all this asks of you. For those minutes, be completely present. No half-reps while thinking about a meeting. No distracted sets. Full attention, full effort, full presence. The transformation you are here for happens in those fully-present moments, not in the ones where your mind is somewhere else.

Built For Kathy

You are building a business, showing up for your kids, and navigating a major hormonal transition, all at the same time. That is a lot to carry. This program is designed to work with your life as it actually is, not a quieter version of it.

Perimenopause changes everything: how you build muscle, how you recover, how your body responds to stress. We work with that, not against it. Every session is purposeful, fits your schedule, and respects your energy. No chest work or planks throughout. Your upper body push days focus on shoulders and triceps. Core work is deep and functional, the kind that actually rebuilds the connection you have noticed weakening.

Priority 01

Rebuild core strength and muscle tone, specifically addressing perimenopausal muscle loss in the midsection

Priority 02

Lower body strength and tone, glutes, legs, and hip stability for longevity and metabolic health

Priority 03

Stress regulation and energy, every session ends with a deliberate wind-down. You train hard, then you let go.

Your Sleep

If you are waking between 3 and 4am and cannot get back to sleep, you are not alone and there is nothing wrong with you. This is one of the most common experiences women describe at this stage of life. This program includes specific tools to support better sleep quality, and you will find them in the notes.

Perimenopause & Muscle

▼

Declining estrogen directly reduces muscle protein synthesis which is why you have noticed changes in your core over the last year. Resistance training with progressive overload is the most evidence-backed intervention for reversing this. This program is built specifically around that science. Consistency over 3–6 months is where the real transformation happens.

Sleep: Why This Stage of Life Affects It and What Actually Helps

▼

Many women going through perimenopause describe the same thing: falling asleep without much trouble, then waking somewhere between 3 and 4am with a mind that immediately starts running. It feels frustrating, but it makes complete sense given where your body is right now. As estrogen and progesterone shift during this transition, the hormonal signals that normally keep sleep deep and steady become less consistent. Your body also tends to release its natural morning wake signal earlier than it used to. Add a high-demand life on top of that and rest becomes genuinely harder to hold onto. The good news is that this responds well to the right habits. This program builds several of them in directly: the breathwork and cool-down at the end of every session are not just recovery tools, they are signals to your nervous system that it is safe to rest. The magnesium glycinate in the notes supports deeper sleep from the inside. And keeping a consistent wake time, even on weekends, is one of the most reliable ways to gradually reset the pattern. This will not change overnight. But with consistent training, better recovery habits, and the sleep tools in these notes, most women notice a real difference within four to six weeks.

Sleep & Cortisol: Practical Tools

▼

At 5–6 hours of sleep per night your cortisol is elevated before you even walk into the gym. This means recovery is harder, fat loss is harder, and muscle building is harder. The breathing and cool down work in this program is not optional, it is a direct intervention on your cortisol levels. Two evidence-based tools to add immediately: first, magnesium glycinate 300–400mg taken 60 minutes before bed. This is the best-absorbed form for sleep quality, supports HPA axis regulation, and directly improves deep sleep in perimenopausal women. Second, set a consistent wake time 7 days a week including weekends. A fixed wake time is the single most effective sleep intervention in the research. It resets your circadian rhythm faster than anything else. Cool dark room. Phone off. Sleep is the mechanism through which your training produces results.

Protein

▼

During perimenopause your protein needs increase. Aim for a minimum of 1.6g per kg of bodyweight per day, ideally closer to 2g. Spread across 3–4 meals. This is non-negotiable for the muscle building this program is designed to deliver.

Pre and Post Workout Nutrition: Training at 6am Changes Everything

▼

Training fasted at 6am with low sleep and elevated cortisol puts your body in a catabolic state before you even walk in. Two simple interventions change this. Pre-workout (15–30 min before): 20–30g fast-digesting protein, Greek yogurt, a protein shake, or cottage cheese. Post-workout (within 30–45 min): 30–40g protein combined with 30–40g carbohydrates. The carbs matter here: post-exercise carbohydrates directly blunt the cortisol spike that follows training. A banana and a protein shake, or rice cakes with Greek yogurt, are ideal. This is not about calories, it is about managing your hormonal environment around training.

Creatine Monohydrate: The Most Important Supplement for This Program

▼

Creatine monohydrate is the most evidence-supported supplement for women in perimenopause pursuing muscle and strength. The research is consistent: 3–5g daily increases lean muscle mass, improves strength output, supports bone mineral density, and has demonstrated benefits for cognitive function during hormonal transition. This is not a bodybuilder supplement. It is the one supplement the science most clearly supports for exactly what this program is trying to do. Take 3–5g daily, timing does not matter, consistency does. Plain creatine monohydrate powder, no added sugar or caffeine. Start alongside Week 1 and do not stop between cycles.

No Chest Work: Upper Body Strategy

▼

All pushing movements have been replaced with shoulder press, tricep, and overhead work. You will build strong, toned arms and defined shoulders without any chest involvement. The pulling work, rows, lat pulldowns, assisted pull-ups, provides the upper body volume and balance the program needs.

Core Work: Why No Crunches or Planks

▼

The core work in this program, dead bugs, bird dogs, Pallof press, McGill curl-ups, suitcase carries, targets the deep core musculature most affected by perimenopause. It rebuilds function and strength without spinal compression or pelvic floor demand. This is the right approach for where your body is right now.

Time: Your Biggest Past Obstacle

▼

Every session fits in 45–55 minutes including warm-up and cool-down. The cool-down is included in that time, not added to it. The schedule is built around your life in Manhattan, not the other way around.

Progressive Overload: Track Your Weights

▼

Write down what you lift every session. The program works because each week you ask slightly more of your body than the week before. Without tracking you are guessing. A simple notes app on your phone is all you need.

Cardiovascular Warm-Up: Every Session

▼

Before every session spend 3–5 minutes on a treadmill walk, light bike, or elliptical. At 47 and training at 6am your core temperature is low and your connective tissue is cold. Loading cold joints increases injury risk dramatically. Keep it light: the goal is to raise your heart rate to 50–55% and break a light sweat before the activation work begins. This is injury prevention, not optional.

RPE: How to Use It

▼

RPE stands for Rate of Perceived Exertion on a scale of 1–10. RPE 6 means you are working but could keep going comfortably. RPE 8 means you have 2 more reps left before failure. RPE 9 means you have 1 rep left. In perimenopause your perceived exertion can vary dramatically week to week depending on sleep, stress, and hormones. The RPE targets in this program are guidelines not rules. If Week 2 loads feel like RPE 9 instead of 7, keep those loads for another week before increasing. Your body is the authority.

Hydration and Electrolytes

▼

Aim for a minimum of 2.5–3 liters of water daily. At your stress level, sleep deprivation, and training volume you are very likely chronically under-hydrated without realizing it. Perimenopausal women lose electrolytes faster than average. Add electrolytes around training, a pinch of sea salt and a squeeze of lemon is enough, or a quality electrolyte supplement with no added sugar. Dehydration directly reduces energy, focus, and strength, three things you are already managing carefully.

Low Energy Days: What to Do

▼

There will be mornings where full training is the wrong choice. If you slept under 4 hours or feel genuinely depleted, do not attempt the full session. Do your activation protocol, 20 minutes of LISS cardio, and go home. This is not failure, it is smart training. Consistency over months matters far more than any single session. Pushing through genuine depletion raises cortisol, increases injury risk, and sets back recovery.

Week 3 Self-Regulation

▼

Week 3 is the most demanding week of the cycle. If Week 2 felt harder than expected or your energy and sleep were particularly poor, you have full permission to repeat Week 2 loads in Week 3 rather than increasing. This extends the cycle to 5 weeks before the deload and is a completely legitimate training decision. Forcing heavier loads on an under-recovered nervous system produces worse results than extending the build phase. More is not always more.

Cardio Strategy

▼

At stress level 8 with 5–6 hours of sleep, high intensity cardio would push cortisol higher and work against your results. Instead this program uses two types of cardio that support your hormonal environment. LISS on Mondays, 30 minutes at 50–60% heart rate, supports fat metabolism without recovery cost. Zone 2 at the end of each Saturday session, 20 minutes at 60–70% heart rate, is the most evidence-backed cardio for perimenopausal metabolic health and longevity. No HIIT until sleep and stress improve.

HRV: Smarter Daily Training Decisions

▼

Heart Rate Variability (HRV) is a morning measurement that reflects how recovered your nervous system is. A free app called HRV4Training measures it using your phone camera, no wearable required. 60 seconds each morning before you get up. When your HRV is below your personal baseline, your body is under-recovered: modify the session or do LISS only. When HRV is at or above baseline, train at full intensity. This removes guesswork and protects you from pushing hard on the days when hard training works against you.

Your Cycle and Your Training

▼

Even in perimenopause, many women still have identifiable cycle phases. During the follicular phase (start of period to ovulation) estrogen rises, this is typically your best window for heavier loads and higher intensity. During the luteal phase (ovulation to period) progesterone dominates, perceived effort increases, and recovery is slower, reduce volume slightly and prioritize protein and sleep even more. Track your energy on a 1–10 scale in your Friday check-in and over time we will identify patterns to make smarter programming decisions together.
