Before You Start

Mariana, open this before your session, find your day, click on it and expand it. Follow it. Put your phone down between sets and stay present. The rest periods in this program are intentional recovery windows, not scrolling time. Being present during your workout is the difference between going through the motions and actually getting results. This is your hour. Protect it.

01 Warm Up Always

Every session starts with 3 to 5 minutes of light cardio. This protects your joints and connective tissue. Do not skip it even when you feel pressed for time.

02 Breathe First

Monday and Thursday sessions start with a short breathing exercise before activation. It takes 2 minutes and makes everything that follows more effective.

03 Exhale on Effort

On every exercise, exhale as you do the hard part. This switches on your deep core automatically on every rep.

04 Hydration

500ml of water before you start. Sip throughout the session. Add a pinch of sea salt or an electrolyte tab if you trained the day before or it is warm. More on this below.

05 Friday Check-In

Send me a voice note or message every Friday. Energy out of 10, key weights, one question. I want to hear how it is going. I am here.

Mariana, This Is Yours

Your goal is to feel lean, fit, and toned. Not bulky. Not overly muscular. Just strong in a way that shows. This program is built around exactly that. The weights you will use and the rep ranges in this program build muscle density and definition not size. You will get leaner, more defined, and stronger. Your clothes will fit differently. That is what this looks like in practice.

The waist changes you have been noticing are not random. At 38, estrogen begins its first gradual decline and this is almost always felt first in how the body distributes fat specifically toward the midsection. Building lean muscle is the most evidence-backed response to this. Muscle raises your metabolism, reshapes your body, and counteracts the hormonal shift. The note section at the bottom of this page explains this in more detail when you are ready to read it.

You train Monday to Thursday, Pilates on Friday, Zone 2 cardio on Saturday, and full rest on Sunday. Every session has a core finisher built in. You will feel this in your abs from week one.

Priority 01

Lean definition throughout glutes, legs, shoulders, arms with visible tone as body composition shifts

Priority 02

Core strength and waist definition deep core work plus a metabolic finisher at the end of every session

Priority 03

Hormonal foundation building muscle now is the single most important investment you can make in how you look and feel at 40, 45, and beyond

Pilates Synergy

Friday Pilates deepens your core activation, improves mobility, and provides active recovery before the weekend

Why Strength Training Will Finally Give You the Results You Have Been Looking For
▼

Mariana, I want to address something you said directly. You have been working out for a while and have not seen results. The most common reason for this is training without structure, without progressive overload, and without a program designed specifically for your body and goals. Going to the gym consistently matters, but if each session is not progressively more demanding than the last, your body has no reason to change. That is not a failure on your part. That is a gap in the approach. This program closes that gap. Every week asks slightly more of your body than the week before. That is the mechanism of change.

On looking muscular: this is the most common fear women have about strength training and it is based on a misunderstanding. The rep ranges and weights in this program build muscle density and definition, not size. Getting visibly larger requires a significant caloric surplus and very high training volume sustained over many years. What this program produces is the lean, toned look you described: muscles that are visible and firm, not bulky. The more muscle you carry, the more calories your body burns at rest and the more defined your body appears.

What Is Happening at 38 and Why Muscle Matters Now
▼

At 38 your estrogen levels are beginning their first gradual decline often called perimenopause, which can begin 8 to 10 years before the final menstrual period. One of the earliest signs is exactly what you have noticed: changes in how the body distributes fat, particularly toward the midsection, even without significant weight change. Estrogen plays a direct role in maintaining muscle mass and directing fat toward the hips and thighs rather than the abdomen. As it declines, body composition shifts without any change in diet or activity. The evidence-backed response to this is resistance training specifically the kind of progressive overload program you are doing. Building lean muscle raises your resting metabolic rate, improves insulin sensitivity, and directly counteracts the hormonal shift. Starting now, at 38, means you are building the foundation that will protect your body composition, bone density, and energy at 45, 50, and beyond. This is the most important investment you can make in your long-term health.

Core Finishers Why Every Session Ends This Way
▼

Every session ends with 8 to 12 minutes of direct core work. This is not optional and not interchangeable with the strength work that came before it. The finishers target all layers of the core systematically: the deep transverse abdominis through dead bugs and Pallof press, the rectus abdominis (the visible abs) through hollow body holds and cable crunches, the obliques through bicycle crunches, Russian twists, and side plank variations, and the lower abs through leg raises and reverse crunches. Doing this at the end of every session, four days per week, across 4 weeks of progressive overload, produces visible change. Consistency matters more than any single session.

Training Around Your Cycle
▼

At 38 with a regular cycle, your hormones fluctuate across the month in ways that directly affect your energy, strength, and recovery. Understanding this helps you train smarter. In the follicular phase (days 1 to 14, from the start of your period): estrogen rises, energy is typically higher, strength and tolerance for hard training is at its peak. This is when to push hardest, increase weights, and take on new challenges. In the luteal phase (days 15 to 28): progesterone rises, body temperature is slightly higher, recovery takes longer, and energy can feel more variable. This is when to maintain rather than push, keep to the program but do not force peak weights if you feel depleted, and prioritize recovery. You do not need to change the program structure just use this awareness to understand why some weeks feel harder than others and self-regulate your RPE accordingly. This is not a limitation. It is information.

RPE and How to Use It
▼

RPE stands for Rate of Perceived Exertion on a scale of 1 to 10. RPE 7 means you have 3 reps left before you could not do another. RPE 9 means you have 1 rep left. Use this to self-regulate your weights week to week. If a week feels significantly harder than the RPE target especially in the luteal phase of your cycle or after poor sleep keep the same weights rather than increasing. Consistency across 4 weeks matters more than any single peak session.

Hydration and Electrolytes During Your Sessions
▼

Mariana, this is one of the simplest things you can do to feel better during your sessions and it is one of the most commonly overlooked. Dehydration of even 1 to 2% of your body weight reduces strength output, focus, and endurance noticeably. You may not feel thirsty and still be dehydrated.

Before your session: Drink 500ml of water in the 30 to 60 minutes before you train. If you are training first thing in the morning, drink it as soon as you wake up before anything else. This is non-negotiable.

During your session: Sip water consistently throughout. 150 to 250ml every 15 to 20 minutes is enough. Keep a bottle within reach at all times. During your rest periods, sip water instead of reaching for your phone.

After your session: Rehydrate within 30 minutes. 400 to 600ml minimum. Add an electrolyte tab or a pinch of sea salt and a squeeze of lemon if you sweated heavily.

When to add electrolytes: Plain water is enough for most sessions. Add electrolytes when you trained the previous day and feel flat, when the session is longer than 45 minutes, when it is hot, or when you wake up feeling low energy before training. Electrolytes include sodium, potassium, and magnesium. Simple options: a pinch of sea salt in water with a banana before training, coconut water after, or a clean electrolyte tab with no added sugar.

Across the day: Aim for 2 to 2.5 litres of water daily minimum. A simple check: your urine should be pale yellow through most of the day. Dark yellow means you are behind on fluids.

During your cycle: In the luteal phase your body temperature is slightly higher and you lose more fluid. Increase your daily intake by 2 to 3 extra glasses during the two weeks before your period. This also helps with bloating, headaches, and the general heaviness that phase can bring.

Creatine Monohydrate: The One Supplement Worth Taking
▼

Creatine monohydrate is the most evidence-supported supplement for women pursuing lean muscle and body recomposition. The research is consistent: 3 to 5g daily improves training output, increases lean muscle mass, supports strength gains, and has no meaningful side effects. It is not a bodybuilder supplement. It is a performance tool that allows you to do more work at a higher quality in every session, which compounds into better results over 4 weeks and beyond. Take 3 to 5g daily in any liquid. Timing does not matter. Consistency does. A plain unflavored powder with no added sugar is all you need. Start from Week 1 and do not stop between cycles.
