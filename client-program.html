Before You Start

You do not need to read this entire document at once. Open it each morning before your session, find your day, and follow it. That is all. Here are the three things that matter most before your first session.

01 Warm Up First

Every session starts with 3 to 5 minutes on the treadmill or bike before anything else. You are training at 6am. Your body needs this especially your joints and connective tissue.

02 Low Energy Days

If you slept under 4 hours or feel genuinely depleted, do your activation and 20 minutes easy cardio only and go home. One modified session protects weeks of progress. Do not push through real exhaustion.

03 Check In Every Friday

Send me a quick message or voice note: your energy this week out of 10, your key weights, and one thing that felt hard or that you have a question about. That is it. This keeps everything on track.

Built For You

This program is designed around where your body is right now not where it was five years ago. Perimenopause changes everything: how you build muscle, how you recover, how your body responds to stress. We work with that, not against it. Every session is purposeful, fits your schedule, and respects your energy.

No chest work or planks throughout. Your upper body push days focus on shoulders and triceps. Core work is deep and functional not crunches, not planks, but the kind of core training that actually rebuilds the connection you've noticed weakening.

Priority 01

Rebuild core strength and muscle tone specifically addressing perimenopausal muscle loss in the midsection

Priority 02

Lower body strength and tone glutes, legs, and hip stability for longevity and metabolic health

Priority 03

Stress regulation and energy every session ends with a deliberate wind-down. You train hard, then you let go.

Perimenopause Note

Higher cortisol sensitivity means we train hard but recover smart. No grinding through exhaustion. Sleep and protein are as important as the sessions themselves.

Program Notes

Perimenopause & Muscle

Declining estrogen directly reduces muscle protein synthesis which is why you have noticed changes in your core over the last year. Resistance training with progressive overload is the most evidence-backed intervention for reversing this. This program is built specifically around that science. Consistency over 3–6 months is where the real transformation happens.

Sleep & Cortisol

At 5–6 hours of sleep per night your cortisol is elevated before you even walk into the gym. This means recovery is harder, fat loss is harder, and muscle building is harder. The breathing and cool down work in this program is not optional it is a direct intervention on your cortisol levels. Prioritizing sleep is as important as the training itself.

Protein

During perimenopause your protein needs increase. Aim for a minimum of 1.6g per kg of bodyweight per day ideally closer to 2g. Spread across 3–4 meals. This is non-negotiable for the muscle building this program is designed to deliver.

No Chest Work Upper Body Strategy

All pushing movements have been replaced with shoulder press, tricep, and overhead work. You will build strong, toned arms and defined shoulders without any chest involvement. The pulling work rows, lat pulldowns, pull ups provides the upper body volume and balance the program needs.

Core Work Why No Crunches or Planks

The core work in this program dead bugs, bird dogs, Pallof press, McGill curl ups, suitcase carries targets the deep core musculature that is most affected by perimenopause. It rebuilds function and strength without spinal compression or pelvic floor demand from planks. This is the right approach for where your body is right now.

Time Your Biggest Past Obstacle

Every session fits in 45–55 minutes including warm up and cool down. The cool down is included in that time not added to it. Wednesday through Saturday, early mornings. The schedule is built around your life, not the other way around.

Progressive Overload Track Your Weights

Write down what you lift every session. The program works because each week you ask slightly more of your body than the week before. Without tracking you are guessing. A simple notes app on your phone is all you need.

Cardiovascular Warm Up Every Session

Before every session spend 3–5 minutes on a treadmill walk, light bike, or elliptical. At 47 and training at 6am your core temperature is low and your connective tissue is cold. Loading cold joints increases injury risk dramatically. This is not optional it is injury prevention. Keep it light: the goal is to raise your heart rate to 50–55% and break a light sweat before the activation work begins.

RPE How to Use It

RPE stands for Rate of Perceived Exertion on a scale of 1–10. RPE 6 means you are working but could keep going comfortably. RPE 8 means you have 2 more reps left in the tank before failure. RPE 9 means you have 1 rep left. In perimenopause your perceived exertion can vary dramatically week to week depending on sleep, stress, and where your hormones are on a given day. The RPE targets in this program are guidelines not rules. If Week 2 loads feel like RPE 9 instead of 7, keep those loads for another week before increasing. Your body is the authority not the number on the plan.

Hydration and Electrolytes

Aim for a minimum of 2.5–3 liters of water daily. At your stress level, sleep deprivation, and training volume you are very likely chronically under-hydrated without realizing it. Perimenopausal women also lose electrolytes faster than average. Add electrolytes to your water around training a pinch of sea salt and a squeeze of lemon is enough, or a quality electrolyte supplement with no added sugar. Dehydration directly reduces energy, focus, and strength three things you are already managing carefully.

Low Energy Days What to Do

There will be mornings where full training is the wrong choice. Use this framework: if you slept under 4 hours, or you feel genuinely depleted before you start, do not attempt the full session. Instead do your full activation protocol, 20 minutes of LISS cardio, and go home. This is not failure it is smart training. Consistency over months matters far more than any single session. Pushing through genuine depletion raises cortisol, increases injury risk, and sets back recovery. Listen to your body. It is usually right.

Week 3 Self-Regulation

Week 3 is the most demanding week of the cycle. If Week 2 felt harder than expected or your energy and sleep were particularly poor, you have full permission to repeat Week 2 loads in Week 3 rather than increasing. This extends the cycle to 5 weeks before the deload and is a completely legitimate training decision. Forcing heavier loads on an under-recovered nervous system produces worse results than extending the build phase. More is not always more.

Weekly Check-In Protocol

Every Friday send me a brief check-in. Voice note or message whichever is easier. This keeps the program responsive to how your body is actually adapting and ensures the next week is built on real information not assumptions.

Cardio Strategy

At stress level 8 with 5–6 hours of sleep, high intensity cardio would push cortisol higher and work against your results. Instead this program uses two types of cardio that support your hormonal environment. LISS (low intensity steady state) on Mondays 30 minutes at 50–60% heart rate supports fat metabolism and cardiovascular health without recovery cost. Zone 2 cardio at the end of each Saturday session 20 minutes at 60–70% heart rate is the most evidence-backed cardio for perimenopausal metabolic health and longevity. No HIIT until sleep and stress improve.
